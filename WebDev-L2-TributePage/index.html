A. P. J. Abdul Kalam

The Missile Man who taught a nation to dream.

15 October 1931 – 27 July 2015

His story

Avul Pakir Jainulabdeen Abdul Kalam was born in Rameswaram, a small island town in Tamil Nadu. His family had little money, and as a boy he delivered newspapers before school to help at home. Those early mornings taught him that discipline matters more than comfort.

He studied physics at St. Joseph's College in Tiruchirappalli and then aeronautical engineering at the Madras Institute of Technology. He wanted to fly, but he chose a different path: building the machines that go into the sky.

At ISRO he led the project that built India's first satellite launch vehicle, the SLV-III. Later, at DRDO, he guided the country's missile programme, which produced Agni and Prithvi. Colleagues remembered him as a leader who shared credit and took blame.

In 2002 he became the 11th President of India. People called him the "People's President" because he kept meeting students and teachers. Even after leaving office he returned to classrooms, and he passed away in 2015 while giving a lecture to students in Shillong.

Key moments

  1. 1931

    Born in Rameswaram, Tamil Nadu.

  2. 1960

    Graduates in aeronautical engineering from the Madras Institute of Technology.

  3. 1969

    Joins ISRO and later leads the SLV-III project.

  4. 1980

    SLV-III places the Rohini satellite in orbit.

  5. 1997

    Receives the Bharat Ratna, India's highest civilian honour.

  6. 1999

    Publishes his autobiography, Wings of Fire.

  7. 2002

    Elected the 11th President of India and serves until 2007.

  8. 2015

    Passes away on 27 July at IIM Shillong, doing what he loved: teaching.
